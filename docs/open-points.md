# Open points before production

Staging runs on placeholders wherever an input is still owed (ADR 0025, item 27). Every placeholder is listed here, with every other point that must be settled before Phase 2 reaches production. Nothing goes to production while an item here is open, except the engineering follow-ups, which say which of them waits on a ruling. When one is settled, record the answer, the date and where it now lives, and move it to "Settled" at the end, keeping its number.

A number is never reused: a new point takes the next free one, which `npm run check:open-points` prints. That check, which CI runs, also fails on a number used twice, on a settled point left in an open table, and on a citation of a point, here or anywhere in the repository, that is not here. The points were renumbered on 27 September 2026; the old numbers are mapped at the end.

How to hand over each answer is in `docs/phase2-inputs.md`.

- **114. Raising an invoice in FSM, and finding it again.** Settled 23 September 2026, both halves, against the owner's own org (`docs/decisions/0055-invoices.md`, `docs/verification.md`).
  - **Raising it.** The API does raise one. The `500 INTERNAL_ERROR` was ours: the create had named the work order alone, and `$Service_Line_Items` and `$finance_data` are mandatory. With all three the same call answers 201. A closed job is now invoiced by the five-minute pass and nobody has to remember.
  - **Finding it.** No listing is needed. The work order's **service line** carries `Invoice_Id` — it is the work order's own top-level fields that do not, which is what the proof saw — and that invoice's `ZBilling_InvoiceId` is the Books ID `books.invoicePdf` wants. It is read the same way whoever raised it, so an invoice the owner raises by hand in FSM's screen is picked up within five minutes.
  - What was left, the invoice being a draft, is settled below (item 115).
- **115. A visit's invoice is issued as it is raised.** Ruled by the owner on 23 September 2026: the invoice is marked sent in Books as part of raising it, so it is a valid tax invoice, and only then may the client see it (`docs/decisions/0056-issuing-the-invoice.md`). The CA's view on GST is still wanted (items 2 and 3), but it does not hold the sending back. Two things follow.
  - **No draft that already exists is ever sent by code**, including one an earlier pass raised and could not send. A draft can be deleted and an issued invoice only voided with a credit note, so the backlog's drafts in the owner's real Books stay the owner's to delete (item 19). **Deal with them by hand before this is deployed.**
  - **A client's advance now settles against their invoice.** ADR 0044's payments pass waits only while an invoice is a draft, so an issued one is paid down on the next five-minute run with no further change.
- **116. Production's Access service token.** `mm-ci-production` has a Service Auth policy on production's Access applications (ops, app, tech), set up by the owner on 22 September 2026. It is kept in `.env.production-access` and as the `production` environment's `CF_ACCESS_CLIENT_ID` and `CF_ACCESS_CLIENT_SECRET` in GitHub; `verify-ci-secrets` checks it against the ops console (`docs/runbook.md`, step 3).
- **117. How long a refund takes.** The app says "5 to 7 working days", Razorpay's time for a normal refund. Ruled 22 September 2026 (ADR 0025, item 28).
- **118. The UPI app on a payment.** The app shows "UPI" alone. Ruled 22 September 2026 (ADR 0025, item 29).
- **119. An invited friend is in the CRM.** Settled 24 September 2026 by making `leads.loss_extent` optional, so every booking leaves a lead whichever page it came from (`docs/decisions/0060-an-invited-friend-reaches-ops-and-the-crm.md`, migration 0031). The landing was not made to ask: it is deliberately short, and a friend arriving on someone else's invite is the visitor a question costs most. Migration 0025 could not do this because it rebuilt the table and `tryon_jobs` points at a lead; 0031 swaps the column in place instead, so `leads` is never dropped. **The trade:** an invited friend's lead carries no loss extent, and Zoho leaves the field empty rather than guessing.
- **120. A referral consultation without self-serve booking is recorded.** Settled 24 September 2026: with `SELF_SERVE_BOOKING` off, `/r/:code` and `/book` no longer answer `409 ops_assisted`. They write the person, the consent, the invite's attribution, the lead and a row in `consultation_requests`, hold no slot, and answer `201` with `state: "requested"`; the page says ops will fix the hour on WhatsApp. Ops see it in the console's task queue, and it leaves that queue when the client has a consultation (`docs/decisions/0060-an-invited-friend-reaches-ops-and-the-crm.md`, migration 0032). - **124. Technicians' phones.** Settled 24 September 2026: any phone the technician already owns, an iPhone included; the owner ruled against buying company Android phones (ADR 0025, item 54). Largely built since (PR #101). **Navigate opens on both platforms:** `apps/tech/src/lib/navigate.ts` uses the Maps URL ADR 0054 chose, preferring the address's pin and falling back to the typed address, where `geo:` had silently done nothing on an iPhone. **An installed iOS app is the mitigation, not the cost:** Apple grant a home-screen web app its own counter of days of use and "do not expect" its data to be deleted, and the app ships a manifest so Add to Home Screen buys both (ADR 0053). **A second cookie jar is left as a second device** rather than merged or auto-revoked (ADR 0053), and the sign-in tells revoked from ended from never-held. What no desktop can answer — whether a real iPhone grants persistence, whether an installed store survives eight days untouched, whether `getUserMedia` yields ten usable frames, whether the session survives an iOS update — is Part 6 of `docs/tech-field-test.md` (item 72). Client addresses on phones we do not own are item 55.
- **126. The no-show wait.** Settled 24 September 2026: 15 minutes for every visit type, a first fit included (`NO_SHOW_WAIT_MIN` in `src/policy/no-show.ts`). "Whether the wait differs for a first fit" is answered: it does not. The config stays one number per type, so a type can be given its own later without touching the rule, and ops change each in the console (Settings · Rules, ADR 0061), 5 to 120 minutes.
- **127. When a job's address unlocks.** Settled 24 September 2026: 6 pm in India on the day before the visit (`UNLOCK_HOUR` and `unlocksAt` in `src/policy/job-visibility.ts`), where midnight stood in before. The unlock follows the message, not the day: the technician sees the address at the hour the client is told someone is coming (item 40), which keeps a whole day's client list off a phone that might be lost. Tomorrow's job is on the technician's list from midnight and its address is not, so `day: "tomorrow"` no longer means unlocked, and the card the app keeps for a basement is cached only from 6 pm (`keepCards`, `apps/tech/src/lib/useDay.ts`). The hour is set in the console (Settings · Rules, ADR 0061), so moving it is not a release. The locked card still reads "The address and the client's card open the day before", six hours early; the owner approves that line with the rest of the copy (item 42).

- **138. The spend on map services.** Settled at ₹0 by the owner's ruling. Nothing is billed today: staging's key is refused (item 54) and production's `GEOCODE_PROVIDER` is `"none"`. With a working key, one metered call per address saved — Geocoding (India) SKU `AB12-DA89-B523`, 70,000 free a month — while autocomplete and the place lookup that closes its session are both unlimited-free SKUs, provided the session token is sent, which `src/providers/google-places.ts` does and a test proves. Four guards keep it there: `GEOCODE_DAILY_CEILING` (200; refuses with `503 busy` rather than spending, `"0"` is the kill switch), `GEOCODE_CEILING_MAX` (1,800, refused at startup), a limit of 120 suggestions a client a day, and the Google Cloud quota the owner sets by hand, the only one that binds if our code is wrong. When address capture is built.** Settled 23 September 2026: built, on staging — the fields (building, flat, floor, tower, landmark), the Place ID, the coordinate and its source, the provider, the ceilings and the client app's combobox. Typing an address by hand stays first-class and saves the same address without a pin. What it left is done too: a check-in with no coordinate to measure against reads as unmeasured, not "0 m" (ADR 0065), and FSM's contact takes the address we hold (ADR 0070). The free coordinate sources, the technician's check-in and the client's own phone, are not built (item 54).
- **140. Google's point-in-polygon clause and the geofence.** Settled by building the conservative reading. The geofence measures against a coordinate from the **Geocoding** API, which Google's terms let us keep indefinitely; the "No Creating Content" clause (ToS 3.2.3(c)(iv)) forbids by example using latitude and longitude **from the Places API** "as an input for point-in-polygon analysis", and none is used so. The prohibition is broader than its example, and this is the one reading in ADR 0054 on which a careful person could differ: if the owner wants it narrower, ask Google whether a proximity check on a Geocoding coordinate counts. Nothing waits on the answer.
