# 0049. DPDP readiness

- Status: accepted. Amended by ADR 0066: an erasure refuses while a visit is booked or a payment held, its files go after its D1 batch, and an address a check-in was measured against is blanked rather than deleted. Amended by ADR 0094: each consent keeps where it was given, which ops see and the export carries, and an erasure blanks a check-in's coordinates.
- Date: 2026-09-22

## Context

P2-M6 is "DPDP readiness (roadmap Stage 4, before 14 May 2027): access and deletion requests end to end, retention jobs verified, and a breach runbook." The plan adds: deletion reaching FSM, the CRM and R2 within 7 days; `audit_log` as the processing record; and a grievance route.

Already in place:

- **Consent per purpose,** with its date and notice version, switched by the client (ADR 0042), and, from 28 September 2026, where it was given (ADR 0094).
- **Correction:** the client edits their address, and changes their number with a code to both numbers and ops' confirmation (ADR 0042).
- **Deletion:** requested in the app, then processed by ops as the Phase 1 erasure (ADR 0019). That deletes try-on photographs and results the same day, blanks the person in D1, and blanks their CRM record by queue.
- **Retention:** try-on photographs and results expire on their own (ADR 0039). The account deletion policy is photographs within 7 days and invoices kept 8 years (`src/policy/account-deletion.ts`).
- **The audit log:** append-only, with each ops call and each client decision about their data (ADR 0031).

## Decision

**Erasure now reaches Phase 2's data** (`erasePerson`).

- **Visit photographs:** deleted from the client-photos bucket (the only way they are ever deleted), and their rows with them.
- **Saved addresses and number-change requests:** deleted.
- **A grievance's words:** blanked.
- **Ops' reasons about the client** (ADR 0072), added 26 September 2026: the review reason of a referral grant they were either side of, and the reason for ruling on a no-show of theirs, are blanked; the decisions stay.
- **Kept, as records:** visits, payments, refunds, credits and the invoices in Books (8 years, by law). The person's row is blanked as before.
- **Kept on the referral, from 26 September 2026 (ADR 0074):** a fitted friend's first name, stored when the grant is made, so the referrer's tracker does not look the friend up. The friend's erasure blanks it, and the tracker then reads "A friend", not "Erased", so it does not tell the referrer the friend asked to be erased (LIFE-13). Whether a first name may stay in the referrer's view after the friend's erasure is counsel's to rule (`docs/open-points.md`, item 63); until then it goes with the rest.
- **FSM:** the contact is anonymised afterwards, as the CRM is.
  - Once a person is erased, the sweeper puts them on the fsm-sync queue, while FSM is connected.
  - The consumer clears the contact's name, numbers and e-mail, and overwrites the service address's street. This was tried on the real org on 22 September 2026: the city stays for the records.
  - A failure is counted, and the sweeper sends it again, up to 5 attempts.
- **The 7 days** run from the client's request to ops' decision.
  - A request that has waited 5 days alerts ops once (`alertAgedDeletions`, on the cron).
  - Processing is then the erasure above, the same day.

**Access** (`GET /api/me/export`): everything held about the client, as a JSON file they download from their profile. It covers their details, addresses, consents with notice versions and, since ADR 0094, where each was given, visits, payments, refunds, credits, referral code, the messages sent to them (kind and date only), grievances and try-ons. Each export is audited (`data.export`).

**Grievances.**

- The client raises one from their profile (`POST /api/grievances`).
- Ops are alerted, by ID only, never with the client's words.
- Ops see the open ones, and close each with their answer (`GET /api/grievances`, `POST /api/grievances/:id/resolve` on the ops surface).
- Raising and answering are audited.

**A breach runbook** (`docs/runbook.md`, "A personal data breach"): contain, assess, notify the Data Protection Board and the people affected, record.

## Consequences

- The app's profile gains "Your data", with a download and a concern. The design has no such card, so its copy is a placeholder.
- Before production, the owner and counsel settle three things (`docs/open-points.md`, item 51):
  - the Grievance Officer named on the privacy page;
  - the time within which grievances are answered (the app says 30 days, a placeholder);
  - the breach runbook's contacts.
